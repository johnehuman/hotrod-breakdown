The Rundown: Week 10

The week's best, worst, luckiest and most avoidable results.

🏆 High Scorer of the Week

Ed — 154.08 points | 11–0 All-Play

Ed put up his best score of the season and the second-highest of 2026, behind only Brett's 157.48. Jahmyr Gibbs (41.20) and George Pickens (38.50) combined for 79.70. The win makes him 9-1, the first 9-1 start in league history, and extends his winning streak to six.

🍀 Lucky Break

Bryan — 82.72 points | 2–9 All-Play

Bryan beat John by 3.96 with the second-lowest score of the week. It's the lowest winning score of 2026, just under Ed's 82.86 in Week 2. Zay Flowers needed 4.45 points on Monday night and got 8.40. Bryan is now 8-1 all-time against John, and at 5-5 he's back in the playoff race.

🎲 Not So Lucky

Castano — 121.50 points | 7–4 All-Play

Castano posted his best score of the season, outscored seven teams, and lost to Josh by 3.92. He's the first team in league history to start 0-10. Starting Alvin Kamara (14.90, bench) over Darnell Washington (1.00) would have won it.

🪑 Bench Blunders

Derrick — 28.64 Points on the Bench

Derrick started Drake Maye (3.02) and left Brock Purdy (31.66) on the bench. With Purdy, he scores 159.46, beats Hannah and posts the highest score of 2026. It was one of three results a bench decision would have flipped. Castano had Kamara. John lost by 3.96 after sitting Seth McGowan (13.70) for Brenton Strange (7.90) and starting the Steelers D/ST, who scored −7. Leaving the defense slot empty would have won it, 85.76 to 82.72. So would any other defense started this week; the lowest scored 3.

🎯 Sharp Shooter

Lewis — 100% Lineup Accuracy

Lewis played a perfect lineup and lost to Kyle, his sixth straight loss. Kyle was next at 97.6%, and his win ended a seven-game skid. Lewis also leads the season race at 94.0%, ahead of Josh (93.3%) and John (92.7%).

Around the League

Two Byes, Two Favorites

Ed (9-1) and Hannah (8-2) hold the byes. In the archive, every team that was 7-3 or better after Week 10 made the playoffs (14 of 14), and 10 of 14 got a bye. Josh is 7-3. No 6-4 team has ever earned a bye (0 of 8), which is the hole Derrick and John are in now.

Bryan's Back

Bryan has the lowest scoring average in the league (83.29) and is 5-5 anyway. He's won two straight, by 0.46 over Kyle and by 3.96 over John, and his playoff odds climbed from 8% to 21%. The schedule helps: Lewis twice, Kyle and John, whose combined record is 13-27. The catch is points. At 832.90 he trails every other team in the race, so he likely needs to finish a full game ahead of the teams he's chasing.

Three-Way Tie for Sixth

Brett, Wayne and Bryan are all 5-5. Brett holds sixth on points (1,115.02), with Wayne 91.64 behind and Bryan 282.12 behind. Dawson (4-6) is a game back and sits 0.36 points ahead of Wayne. History says 5-5 is a coin flip: 8 of 15 made it, and none got a bye.

Lewis's Last Stand

Lewis played a perfect lineup in Week 10 and still lost, his sixth straight. He's 2-8 and is eliminated with a loss to Bryan this week. Even winning out to 6-8, the best he can do is tie for sixth. History isn't kind either: no team that was 3-7 or worse after Week 10 has made the playoffs (0 of 13), a group that also includes Kyle (3-7) and Castano (0-10).

Week 10 Storylines

Ed's Record Watch

Ed has won six straight. The league record is seven, shared by five managers: John (2021), Thomas (2023), Dawson (2023–24), Ed (2025) and Bryan (2025). A win over Kyle this week ties it and clinches a playoff spot. A win over Dawson in Week 12 would make him the first manager to win eight in a row.

The Ed Effect, Week 10

Wayne scored 94.12 against Ed, 9.1 points under his average in his other games. It wasn't a season low, but it extends the pattern: 19 of 24 Ed opponents since 2025 have scored below their norm, by 15.1 points on average. It also broke the Week 10 tradition. Wayne and Ed had traded wins every year since 2022, and it was Wayne's turn. Ed won again and leads the series 4-2.

McCaffrey Takes the Single-Game High

Christian McCaffrey scored 44.80 for Dawson, the most by any player this season. Gibbs's 41.20 had taken the record a few hours earlier, passing Josh Allen's 40.82 from Week 2. It's the best regular-season game since Gibbs's 50.40 in Week 12 of 2025, and the best by anyone else since Jonathan Taylor's 48.60 two weeks before that. Dawson beat Brett for the fourth straight time and leads their series 6-1.

John's Worst Week

John scored 78.76, the week's lowest and his season low. The Steelers D/ST scored −7, the worst by any defense this season and tied for the third-worst by a starting defense since 2022. He's now 1-8 all-time against Bryan. His bye odds fell from 26% to 7%, and he's 6-4 despite the third-most points.

The Schedule That Wasn't

The 2026 schedule was changed by hand before Week 1. The original was last season's schedule, slot for slot, and with the same scores it would look very different. John would be 3-7 instead of 6-4. Dawson would be 5-5 and holding sixth by 0.36 points, not 4-6. Derrick would be 8-2, Hannah 6-4 and Castano 2-8. Ed and Wayne would have met in Week 10 either way.

Movers

Hannah's bye odds jumped 21 points to 52%, the league's biggest gain. Derrick's fell 23 points to 13%, the biggest drop, and John's fell 18. Bryan's playoff odds rose from 8% to 21%. Brett (−14) and Wayne (−12) lost the most.

How this works

Odds come from 400,000 simulations of weeks 11–14 on the real remaining schedule. Each team's scoring level starts from this season's average, then gets pulled strongly toward the league average. In our 2021–2025 archive, only about a fifth of a team's first-half scoring edge carried into weeks 9–14, so hot starts are expected to cool off. Weekly scores swing by about ±22 points, the league's historical spread.

Seeding uses record, then points-for. Divisions only affect the schedule, not seeding, so ESPN's standings page can show a different order.

The model was tested against all five past seasons, week by week. Its odds for the points leader holding on matched what happened (45% predicted, 43% actual).

Win chances come from this simulation. ESPN projections are the sum of each team's current starters, retrieved before release, and lineups can still change. Odds over time show each week's published odds. The model doesn't account for injuries for any team; John is without Puka Nacua (IR) through Week 13, which would lower his odds. These are estimates, not guarantees. Treat any number as about ±2 points.

The Rundown: Week 9

The week's best, worst, luckiest and most avoidable results.

🏆 High Scorer of the Week

Brett — 157.48 points | 11–0 All-Play

Brett posted the highest score of 2026, passing Derrick's 150.18 from Week 3. Jordan Love (36.18) and Kenneth Walker III (34.80) combined for 70.98. With John's 143.66 on the other side, the game totaled 301.14, a new 2026 high one week after Ed and Derrick set it at 288.06, and the fourth-highest combined score in the archive. The win moves Brett to 5-4 and into sixth.

🍀 Lucky Break

Bryan — 86.00 points | 5–6 All-Play

Bryan beat Kyle by 0.46, the second-closest game of 2026. He's been on one side of both of the season's closest finishes: he lost to Ed by 0.04 in Week 2. He won it with Pat Bryant's 13.60 on his bench. Kyle played his best possible lineup and still lost.

🎲 Not So Lucky

John — 143.66 points | 10–1 All-Play

John outscored 10 teams and lost to the one that scored the season high. His 143.66 is the highest losing score of 2026, breaking the record Derrick set a week earlier (140.56), and the third-highest losing score in the archive. His best possible lineup, 154.76, would still have lost by 2.72. He falls to 6-3 and fourth.

🪑 Bench Blunders

None That Mattered

For the second time this season (Week 6 was the other), no bench decision changed a result: every losing team's best possible lineup still lost. The biggest miss came in a win. Bryan started Zay Flowers (3.80) over Pat Bryant (13.60). Derrick left Blake Corum's 11.80 on the bench for D'Andre Swift's 3.10.

🎯 Sharp Shooter

Kyle — 100% Lineup Accuracy

Kyle played a perfect lineup and lost by 0.46, his seventh straight loss. Hannah was next at 99.7%. In the season race, Lewis retook the lead at 93.3%. John and Josh, who led after Week 8, are a hair behind at 93.1%.

Around the League

Five Teams, Three Spots Settled

Ed (8-1) and Hannah (7-2) hold the byes, with Derrick, John and Josh tied at 6-3. In the archive, all 16 teams that were 6-3 or better after Week 9 made the playoffs. Byes are another story: 5 of 9 teams at 6-3 got one, and 4 of 6 at 7-2. Among the 6-3 group, points decide the order: Derrick 1,160.08, John 1,061.14, Josh 1,010.96.

Hannah's Record and Points Don't Match

Hannah is 7-2 with the seventh-most points (927.78). Her all-play record is 45-54, and she's the league's luckiest team, about 2.9 wins ahead of what her scores would predict. After Week 9 she fell 1.48 points behind Wayne, so she'd lose a tiebreaker to him, and she's 104.64 behind Brett. She plays Derrick this week, Wayne in Week 12 and Brett in Week 13.

Sixth Place: Brett by 103

Brett and Wayne are both 5-4. Brett holds sixth on a 103.16-point tiebreaker. History likes 5-4 after Week 9 (10 of 13 made the playoffs) but has never given one a bye. Wayne has won three straight. Bryan (4-5) is a game back; 4 of 17 teams at 4-5 made it.

History Closes the Door

No team that was 3-6 or worse after Week 9 has made the playoffs (0 of 14). That covers Dawson (3-6), Lewis and Kyle (2-7) and Castano (0-9). Castano is the first 0-9 team in league history. His nine straight losses are the longest single-season losing streak in the archive, and 10 straight counting the end of 2025. Kyle has lost seven straight, Lewis five.

Week 9 Storylines

The Ed Effect Strikes Again

Josh scored 67.18 against Ed, his season low and 50.8 points under his average in his other games. It's a pattern. Since 2025, 17 of 23 Ed opponents have scored below their norm, by 15.4 points on average. Ten of 21 set a new season low to date, about 2.4 times the usual rate, and seven posted their season low. The breakdown by manager is below.

Wayne at Ed, Again

Wayne and Ed have met in Week 10 five seasons running, and they've traded wins every year: Wayne in 2022, Ed in 2023, Wayne in 2024, Ed last year. If the pattern holds, it's Wayne's turn. It's also the league's two most extreme opponents. Ed's opponents score 15.4 under their norm. Wayne's score 8.9 over it, the most of any current manager. The stakes are lopsided: Wayne's playoff odds are 65% with a win and 35% with a loss.

Derrick Is on Record Pace

Derrick has 1,160.08 points, the most through nine weeks in the archive, passing Bryan's 1,139.74 from last season. His 98.94-point lead gives him an 87% chance at the points title. Bryan led by 92.16 at this point last year, and Wayne caught him.

Hannah's Tiebreaker Problem

If Hannah loses to Derrick and Brett and Wayne both win, she's 7-3 and they're 6-4, and she trails both on points. That scenario cuts her playoff odds from 92% to 81%. Nine wins would still be safe in every simulation. At 8-6 she makes it 60% of the time, and she wins a tie with Wayne only 18% of the time and with Brett almost never.

Brett Up, Josh Down

Brett's playoff odds rose 18 points to 68%, the league's biggest gain. Josh's bye odds fell from 41% to 18%, the biggest drop. John scored 143.66 and still lost 12 points of bye odds.

How this works

Odds come from 400,000 simulations of weeks 10–14 on the real remaining schedule. Each team's scoring level starts from this season's average, then gets pulled strongly toward the league average. In our 2021–2025 archive, only about a fifth of a team's first-half scoring edge carried into weeks 9–14, so hot starts are expected to cool off. Weekly scores swing by about ±22 points, the league's historical spread.

Seeding uses record, then points-for. Divisions only affect the schedule, not seeding, so ESPN's standings page can show a different order.

The model was tested against all five past seasons, week by week. Its odds for the points leader holding on matched what happened (45% predicted, 43% actual).

Win chances come from this simulation. ESPN projections are the sum of each team's current starters, retrieved before release, and lineups can still change. Odds over time show each week's published odds. The model doesn't account for injuries for any team; John is without Puka Nacua (IR) for Weeks 10–13, which would lower his odds. These are estimates, not guarantees. Treat any number as about ±2 points.

The Rundown: Week 11

The week's best, worst, luckiest and most avoidable results.

🏆 High Scorer of the Week

Lewis — 125.66 points | 11–0 All-Play

Lewis had the week's top score, led by Patrick Mahomes (24.96), Javonte Williams (22.60) and Jameson Williams (17.30). The win over Bryan snapped a six-game losing streak, but at 3-8 the best Lewis can do now is tie for sixth. It was the second-lowest weekly high of the season, behind only Week 6's 125.04.

🍀 Lucky Break

Derrick — 86.00 points | 3–8 All-Play

Derrick's first score under 100 this season, and his low by more than 20 points, still beat Brett by 6.26. It took the trade reversal to get there. With Parker Washington and Emmett Johnson, Brett would have scored about 94.74. Ja'Marr Chase managed 5.60 on 21.26 projected.

🎲 Not So Lucky

Castano — 117.16 points | 8–3 All-Play

Castano posted the week's fourth-best score and his second-best of the season, and lost to Wayne by 2.64. Dak Prescott scored 35.90 against him. It's his 12th straight loss, the longest streak in league history, and 11 of them came this season, also a record.

🪑 Bench Blunders

Kyle — 22.10 Points on the Bench

Kyle left Ladd McConkey (18.20) on the bench and started DK Metcalf (0.70) and Garrett Wilson (5.40). His 74.0% lineup accuracy was the week's worst, though even a perfect lineup (84.90) loses to Ed. No bench decision flipped a result this week. Bryan sat Cam Ward (22.22) for Bo Nix (11.50) and would still have lost, 117.82 to 125.66. Wayne left Jacory Croskey-Merritt (14.70) on the bench and won anyway.

🎯 Sharp Shooter

Castano — 100% Lineup Accuracy

Castano played a perfect lineup and lost anyway. Derrick was also at 100%, but only because his bench scored zero. Lewis still leads the season race at 94.3%, ahead of Josh (93.6%) and John (93.0%).

Around the League

One Bye, Four Teams

Ed has the first bye all but locked: a win over Dawson clinches it. The second is Josh's to lose. He and Hannah are both 8-3, and he holds the points tiebreaker by 86.36. In the archive, 8 of 9 teams that were 8-3 after Week 11 got a bye, but only one of these two can. Derrick and John (7-4) are a game back; 7-4 teams have gotten a bye 2 times in 8.

Brett and Wayne, Kingmakers

Brett and Wayne are fighting each other for the last playoff spot, and they decide the bye race along the way. Wayne plays Hannah this week and Josh in Week 13. Brett gets Josh this week and Hannah in Week 13. Each of those four games moves every bye contender's odds.

Sixth Place Changes Hands

Wayne (6-5) moved into sixth for the first time since Week 2. Brett (5-6) is out of the field for the first time since Week 5, and his 51.58-point edge on Wayne only matters if they tie. History says 6-5 is a 7-in-12 shot and 5-6 is 5 in 12. Bryan (5-6) is also a game back, but at 940.00 points he'd lose any tie.

What They're Thankful For

Ed: the Ed Effect, 20 opponents in 25 games below their average. Derrick: the veto. Without it, he loses to Brett by about 8.7. Wayne: Dak Prescott's 35.90 and a 2.64-point margin. Josh: an 86.36-point tiebreaker cushion over Hannah. Bryan: 5-6 still counts as alive (6%). Castano: the regular season is only 14 weeks.

Playing Out the String

Castano is eliminated. Lewis and Kyle (3-8) can only tie for sixth at best, and either is out entirely with a loss, a Wayne win or a Brett win this week. Dawson (4-7) is at 1.5% and is eliminated if Ed beats him and Wayne or Brett wins. No team that was 4-7 or worse after Week 11 has made the playoffs (0 of 18).

Week 11 Storylines

The Trade That Didn't Count

A trade between Brett and Castano went through before Sunday's games, was vetoed, and ESPN reset both rosters and their Week 11 scores. It decided one game. With Parker Washington (14.80) and Emmett Johnson (22.80), Brett scores about 94.74 and beats Derrick's 86.00. With his own roster back, he lost by 6.26. Had the trade stood, Brett's playoff odds would be 67% instead of 36%, and Derrick's 86% instead of 99%. Castano lost to Wayne either way.

Ed's Record Watch

Ed beat Kyle for his seventh straight win, tying the league record. He and John are the only managers to do it twice. At 10-1, he's off to the best start in league history. A win over Dawson this week makes him the first manager to win eight in a row and clinches a bye.

The Ed Effect, Week 11

Kyle scored 62.80 against Ed, 35.7 points under his average in his other games and his season low by 13.7. That's 20 of 25 Ed opponents since 2025 who scored below their norm, by 15.9 points on average, and 11 of 23 who set a new season low to date.

Derrick Wins Ugly

Derrick hadn't scored under 106 all season. His 86.00 still won, and he's now 1,376.90 points through 11 weeks, the most in league history (Bryan had 1,343.34 last season). His lead over Ed fell from 76.66 to 60.32, the smallest it's been since Week 2. It's still the biggest lead after Week 11 in the archive.

Josh Takes the Second Seed

Josh beat Hannah 117.96–103.96 in their first meeting. Chuba Hubbard (26.50) and Jared Goff (20.36) led the way. Both are 8-3, and Josh leads on points. Hannah's bye odds fell from 52% to 26%, his rose from 32% to 44%. They meet again in Week 14.

Movers

Wayne's playoff odds jumped 24 points to 60%, the biggest gain of the week. Brett's fell 19 points to 36%, and Bryan's fell from 21% to 6%. John climbed from 89% to 98%, and Dawson dropped to 1.5%.

How this works

Odds come from 400,000 simulations of weeks 12–14 on the real remaining schedule. Each team's scoring level starts from this season's average, then gets pulled strongly toward the league average. In our 2021–2025 archive, only about a fifth of a team's first-half scoring edge carried into weeks 9–14, so hot starts are expected to cool off. Weekly scores swing by about ±22 points, the league's historical spread.

Seeding uses record, then points-for. Divisions only affect the schedule, not seeding, so ESPN's standings page can show a different order.

The model was tested against all five past seasons, week by week. Its odds for the points leader holding on matched what happened (45% predicted, 43% actual).

Win chances come from this simulation. ESPN projections are the sum of each team's current starters, retrieved before release, and lineups can still change. Odds over time show each week's published odds. Week 11 uses the final scores after the Brett–Castano trade was reversed. The model doesn't account for injuries for any team; John is without Puka Nacua and Jeremiyah Love, which would lower his odds. These are estimates, not guarantees. Treat any number as about ±2 points.
